# Commuter Tapes

**Read music, know every note on the guitar, and learn music theory, in short lessons you can finish between train stops.**

**[▶ Try it in your browser](https://christinatrujillo.github.io/commuter-tapes/)**. On Android, open it in Chrome and choose **Install app**. Once installed, it works offline.

Commuter Tapes is set in an analog recording studio. Each topic is a cassette on your tape rack, each lesson is a track you record, and the tapes are grouped into volumes, one per chapter. Your progress shows up on VU meters, CLIP lights and LCD readouts. There are no accounts and no ads, and nothing leaves your phone.

## Curriculum

There's one path through the rack. Reading follows method-book order: the staff and note values first, then first position string by string from the high E string down, with a new rhythm idea on each string. Fretboard tapes take each string up the neck once it's been read. From Volume 3 on, the volumes follow the usual order of a guitar theory course.

## How it teaches

- **Rehearsal before each take.** The first time you record a track, a short rehearsal teaches what's new. Each new note, rhythm, shape or theory idea gets a card with an example, how it sounds, and how it connects to what you already know. Cards come two at a time, each pair followed by a quick check with no penalty, then a mixed check before you record. Tracks with nothing new get a quick warm-up instead.
- **Recording a take.** A take is 6 to 15 questions, usually 10 or 12. The tape winds from reel to reel as you answer. Each mistake lights a red **CLIP** lamp, and three clips ruin the take. Finished takes are stamped **Master take** (no clips), **Keeper** (one) or **Rough take**. Missed questions come back at the end. Practice sessions (jams, Trouble spots, Today’s mixtape) have no clips and always run to the end.
- **Spaced repetition, built into practice.** Everything you answer gets a next-review date: tomorrow, then about 3 days, 8, 20 and so on. Intervals stretch less if you were slow, and a miss comes back tomorrow. Answering before it's due doesn't count, so cramming can't fake it. Due material from earlier tapes is mixed into takes (3 of 10 questions, tagged "↺ Review · from tape N"), and missing one costs no clip. Takes alone can't keep up with everything that comes due, so when a dozen or more are waiting, the end of a take offers **Today’s mixtape**, a 12-question review session.
- **Speed-based mastery.** Every answer is timed against a fluency target, for example about 2s to name a note on the neck and 4s to name a note in a scale. Each note or idea is *learning* until you get it right reliably, then *accurate*, and *fluent* only when you're right **and** fast. Fluency fades if something goes long past its review date. Slow answers come up more often, just like wrong ones.
- **Words explained where you meet them.** Music words on cards, in feedback and in liner notes are underlined; tap one and a small liner-note card gives its meaning in a sentence or two.

These follow well-studied learning principles: retrieval practice (recalling instead of rereading), spacing, interleaving (mixing old and new material), small steps with worked examples, feedback that explains every answer, and practicing to fluency rather than stopping at "correct".

## Exercises

- **Reading pitch.** Name a note on the staff or find where it's played; accidentals and the measure rule; key signatures (name the key, or name or find a note in it); steps, skips and leaps; short phrases played in order on the fretboard; road signs: the order a score's measures are played in, and where to go after a measure.
- **Reading rhythm.** Name a note value or rest, count its beats, complete a measure, tap the note on a given count, and **tap a rhythm** in time after a one-measure count-in, on the big pad or with Space on a laptop. Time signatures run from 4/4, 3/4 and 2/4 to cut time and 6/8, and rhythms add triplets once they're taught. The metronome is a click or, on phones, a vibration.
- **The fretboard.** Name a marked note, find a note on one string or everywhere on the neck, tap an interval or octave above a root, and find a mode's home in its parent's major scale pattern, or name the mode from the notes lit as home; complete an arpeggio shape, name an arpeggio from its shape, and add the 7th to a triad's shape.
- **Theory.** Half or whole step, on the neck or the staff; the notes of any major or natural minor scale, spelled correctly; scale degrees in patterns on the neck; how many sharps or flats a key has, and the next key around the circle; relative major and minor keys; naming intervals from note names or the staff; turning intervals upside down; spelling all four kinds of triad and telling them apart; naming triads on the staff, in a key and inverted; naming triad shapes on the neck and tapping a shape's missing note; the chords in any major key, by name and Roman numeral; I, IV and V, and their roots on the neck; spelling and naming common progressions; reading and writing chord symbols; spelling 7th, sus, add9 and 6 chords and telling them apart; the 7th chords in a key; naming chords from chord diagrams and the CAGED shapes; the chords in any minor key; harmonic minor's raised 7th, V7 and diminished 7th; spelling and naming minor progressions; telling whether a song is in the major key or its relative minor; spelling the minor and major pentatonic and the blues scale; the five pentatonic patterns, their roots and the CAGED shape inside each; reading a 12-bar blues chart, its chords in a key, the quick change, the turnaround and minor blues; the seven modes, where each starts in the major scale, and its parent scale in any key; each mode's color note, and spelling modes; the mode on each chord of a key, home chords and modal vamps; melodic minor's raised 6th and 7th, spelling it, and telling the three minor scales apart; the whole-tone and diminished scales: which ones share their notes, which one a string's frets make, and the two chords inside each; each chord's family in a key, the notes a chord and its stand-in share, and which chord can stand in for another; secondary dominants: naming V7/ii and the rest, where one leads, its outside note, and spotting one in a progression; borrowed chords: building iv, ♭VI, ♭VII and ♭III from the parallel minor, naming them, and spotting one in a progression; tritone subs: finding ♭II7, the notes it shares with V7, and swapping it into a ii–V–I.
- **Ear training.** Hear two notes, rising, falling or together, and name the interval, or tap where the second note is on the neck. Hear a triad, one note at a time or together, and name its kind. Hear a progression and tell whether it ends at home, whether the second chord is IV or V, or which progression it is. Hear a 7th chord and name its kind. Hear a progression and tell whether its key is major or minor, or whether a minor key's V chord is minor or major. Hear a scale and tell major pentatonic from minor, or whether it has the blue note; hear the start of a blues and tell whether it makes the quick change. Hear the home chord and a scale, and tell Dorian from natural minor, Mixolydian or Lydian from major, or Phrygian from natural minor; hear a two-chord vamp and name its mode. Hear the home chord and a minor scale and tell natural, harmonic or melodic minor; hear a scale and tell whole-tone from diminished. Hear a progression and tell whether it went home or to a deceptive cadence, whether a chord was swapped for a secondary dominant, whether IV turned minor, and whether V7 or its tritone sub led home. Each question plays as it appears, with **▶ Hear again**. Intervals start very different (octave or 5th?) and narrow down to ones that sound alike, with familiar melodies on the lesson cards as memory aids. The mode cards name well-known songs in each mode, too.

## The app

- **Where to start.** A brand-new learner is asked one question first: start from the beginning (reading music and the neck), or go straight to theory at Vol. 3, for people who already read music and know the neck. It can be changed in Settings, and every tape stays on the rack.
- **Interrupted takes.** A take in progress is saved as it goes, so if the tab closes or the page reloads, opening the same track again that day picks up at the question you were on.
- **First-run guide.** The first time the app opens, a few quick bubbles point out the four tabs and the deck's two buttons (Play next track and Load tape), then leave you on the home screen. After that, a one-time tip explains each new screen the first time you reach it: a tape, a rehearsal, a take. Skip the tour at any step, or replay it all from Settings.
- **Tapes (home).** A tape deck holding the tape with your next track (marked **Now playing**), with the rack below: a wooden shelf per volume, each tape lit with a green light per recorded track. Tap a tape to load it into the deck; a sealed one shows there which tape to finish first. **▶ Play next track** starts your next track straight from the deck; **⏏ Load tape** slides the tape's paper insert up from under the cassette, with the track list, liner notes (tips) and pictures; the reels keep turning above it.
- **Jam.** **Today’s mixtape**, a review session to play about once a day, for what the takes don’t get to; a 60-second sprint with a personal best for each kind (Keys and Intervals sprints unlock as you learn them); **Trouble spots**, twelve questions on whatever you miss most or answer slowest, fretboard and theory alike; **Theory jam**, one-tap mixes from the theory tracks you've recorded (steps and scales, keys, intervals, triads, chords in a key, building chords, minor keys, blues and pentatonics, modes, and ear training), which appears once you reach Vol. 3; and a custom **Jam setup** for fretboard and staff drills.
- **Fretboard.** A reference neck. Show the natural notes only, or every note named with **sharps** or with **flats** (the same frets under their two names). Tap a note to hear it and see where it's written on the staff, or **solo** one note to see everywhere it appears. Once you reach the scale and interval tapes, a **Scale** view lights up any key you've learned (with note names or degrees) and an **Interval** view shows every spot an interval above a note you tap. The Scale view adds the major and minor pentatonic and the blues scale as you learn them, the five pentatonic patterns one at a time, and, from the Modes tape on, all seven modes in each key you've learned. From the triad tapes on, an **Arpeggio** view lights up every note of a chord on any root (with note names or degrees), each chord named the way it's written there (D♭ minor is C♯m); it adds each triad, then each 7th chord, as you learn them.
- **Meters.** Fluent spots and accuracy on LCD readouts (your streak and note count are on the strip at the top of every tab); a level bar per skill (fretboard, reading, rhythm, scales and keys, intervals, triads, chords, ear) showing how much of what you've practiced is fluent; and a signal map of the fretboard: red is still learning, amber is right but slow, green is fluent.
- **Settings.** Grouped as Sound (sound, vibration, metronome, ear training), Lessons (auto-advance, starting at theory, unsealing every tape, daily goal, showing the guide again), Fretboard (left-handed mode, string order when the neck is sideways) and Your tapes (backup and restore).
- **Monitors.** Ear training needs headphones, so ear-training tapes and tracks are marked with a pair of them. On any listening question, **Can’t listen now** pauses listening questions for 15 minutes, after which they come back by themselves; the **MONITORS** switch on each ear-training tape's page turns them back on sooner. While they're off, ear-training tracks wait and listening review is skipped, and for the rest of that day your next track is the first one that doesn't need sound: ear-training tracks never hold up the ones after them. Turning ear training off in Settings skips those tracks for good.

The fretboard stands upright like a chord chart when the phone is upright, and turns sideways like tab when you rotate it. With Reduce Motion turned on in your phone's settings, the app's animations are switched off.

## Privacy

Commuter Tapes has no accounts. Progress is stored in your browser's local storage, so each device and browser keeps its own, and clearing the browser's site data erases it. An iPhone can also clear it by itself if the app isn't installed and goes unopened for about a week, and finishing a volume offers a backup. To keep a copy or move to another device, use **Settings → Export backup** and **Load backup**. The backup is a file you keep; nothing is uploaded.

## Tech

- React, TypeScript and Vite, styled with Tailwind CSS
- [VexFlow](https://www.vexflow.com/) for sheet music. The fretboard is a hand-built SVG.
- Guitar tones synthesized in the browser (Karplus–Strong), so there are no audio files
- Zustand for state, saved to local storage
- Installable PWA with a custom service worker for offline use
- Vitest and Testing Library: music logic, every track in the curriculum, a click-through of the whole app, and a check that no symbol in the app turns into an emoji on iPhones
- Playwright: end-to-end tests of the production build in desktop Chrome and on emulated iPhone and Android phones, with an axe accessibility scan

## Run locally

Requires Node.js 22.22+ or 24.15+.

```sh
git clone https://github.com/christinatrujillo/commuter-tapes.git
cd commuter-tapes
npm install
npm run dev
```

| Command | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `npm run dev` | Start the dev server (add `-- --host` to open it from a phone on the same Wi-Fi) |
| `npm test` | Run the unit tests |
| `npm run test:e2e` | Run the end-to-end tests (see [Testing](#testing)) |
| `npm run typecheck` | Type-check without building |
| `npm run build` | Type-check and build into `dist/` |
| `npm run preview` | Serve the production build locally |

Installing the app and using it offline need HTTPS, so they work on a deployed copy but not on the dev server.

## Testing

`npm test` runs the unit tests with Vitest and Testing Library, in a simulated browser. They cover the music logic, play every track in the curriculum, and click through the whole app.

`npm run test:e2e` runs the end-to-end tests with Playwright, against the production build, in desktop Chrome and on an emulated iPhone (WebKit) and Android phone (Chromium). They check what only a real browser can show:

- the first run: picking where to start, and the tour
- recording a take: a clean one, a ruined one, and one picked up again after a reload
- the tabs, the settings and the browser's Back button
- the fretboard and its sheet music
- exporting a backup, erasing everything and loading the backup again
- opening offline from the service worker's cache (Chromium only)
- an axe accessibility scan of each main screen

Before the first run, download the test browsers with `npx playwright install`. Playwright builds and serves the app by itself. `npx playwright show-report` opens the last run's report, and `npx playwright test --ui` lets you watch the tests and step through them. Results and reports go to `test-results/` and `playwright-report/`, which git ignores.

## Deploy your own

Pushes to `main` run the tests, build the site and publish it to GitHub Pages (`.github/workflows/deploy.yml`). If you fork the repo, turn on **Settings → Pages → Source: GitHub Actions**. The build uses relative paths, so it works at any URL.

## Project structure

```
src/
  music/        notes and fretboard math, rhythm, reading rules and keys (score.ts), scales, intervals, triads, chords and the chords in a key (theory.ts), chord shapes (chordShapes.ts), and the curriculum (curriculum.ts)
  game/         question generation, rehearsal cards, theory, interval, triad, chords-in-a-key, chord-building, minor-key, blues, mode, melodic minor, symmetric scale, arpeggio, chord swap and road sign questions and cards, the glossary, spaced-repetition review and trouble spots, jams and drills
  components/   fretboard, staff and score (with repeats, endings and jumps), rhythm notation, tap pad, circle of fifths, chord diagrams and chord charts, monitors switch, glossary popups, cassette and studio-hardware UI
  screens/      home (deck and rack), a tape's paper insert, rehearsal, recording a take, sprint, jam room, fretboard, meters, settings
  store/        saved progress, review schedule, streaks and settings
  audio/        plucked-string synthesis and metronome
  lib/          hash router, plus date, color, random-number and text helpers
  test-helpers.ts  helpers shared by the tests
e2e/            Playwright end-to-end tests, with saved-progress fixtures and a page object for recording a take
public/         icons and web app manifest
.github/        GitHub Pages deploy workflow
```

**Adding content:** tapes and their tracks are defined in `TAPES` in `src/music/curriculum.ts`, and `VOLUMES` places them on the rack and lists the planned ones. Theory tracks list the ideas they teach in `topics`; each idea's card and quick checks are in `src/game/theory.ts` (intervals and ear training in `src/game/intervals.ts`, triads in `src/game/triads.ts`, chords in a key and progressions in `src/game/harmony.ts`, building chords in `src/game/chords.ts`, minor keys in `src/game/minor.ts`, blues and pentatonics in `src/game/blues.ts`, modes in `src/game/modes.ts`, melodic minor and the Vol. 11 scales in `src/game/scales.ts`, arpeggio shapes in `src/game/arpeggios.ts`, chord swaps in `src/game/swaps.ts`, road signs in `src/game/roads.ts`). Keep track ids unchanged once released, since saved progress is keyed by them. The tests check that every track produces valid questions and that no track reads in a key before its key signature is taught. Fluency targets are set in `FLUENT_MS` in `src/game/questions.ts`.
