# Commuter Tapes

**Read music, know every note on the guitar, and learn music theory, in short lessons you can finish between train stops.**

**[▶ Try it in your browser](https://christinatrujillo.github.io/commuter-tapes/)**. On Android, open it in Chrome and choose **Install app**. Once installed, it works offline.

Commuter Tapes is set in an analog recording studio. Each topic is a cassette on your tape rack, each lesson is a track you record, and the tapes are grouped into volumes, one per chapter. Your progress shows up on VU meters, CLIP lights and LCD readouts. There are no accounts and no ads, and nothing leaves your phone.

## Curriculum

There's one path through the rack. Reading follows method-book order: the staff and note values first, then first position string by string from the high E string down, with a new rhythm idea on each string. Fretboard tapes take each string up the neck once it's been read. From Volume 3 on, the volumes follow the usual order of a guitar theory course.

| Volume | What it covers | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Vol. 1 · Reading & the Neck** | The staff, note values and counting in 4/4 and 3/4; rests, eighth notes, ties and dotted notes; reading all six strings in first position; the high E, low E and A strings up the neck | Available |
| **Vol. 2 · The Whole Neck** | Sharps, flats and naturals, and how long an accidental lasts; the D, G and B strings up the neck; the whole neck, naturals and accidentals; reading by steps, skips and leaps, and short phrases; 2/4 and cut time; reading up the neck; octaves | Available |
| **Vol. 3 · Scales & Keys** | Whole and half steps; the major scale (its steps, scale degrees and spelling in any key); major scale patterns on the neck; key signatures; the circle of fifths, all 15 keys; relative minor keys and the natural minor scale | Available |
| **Vol. 4 · Intervals** | Interval shapes on the neck; naming intervals by number and quality (major, minor, perfect, augmented, diminished); intervals on the staff, in a key and stacked; inversions; ear training, from octaves and 5ths down to 2nds and the tritone | Available |
| **Vol. 5–12** | Triads, chords in a key, building chords, minor keys, blues and pentatonics, modes, more scales and arpeggios, chord substitution | Planned |
| **Bonus Vol. · Reading Extras** | Repeats and road signs, 6/8 and triplets, reading TAB | Planned |

Planned tapes sit on the rack as blank **Coming soon** cassettes. They're numbered too, so a tape's number never changes when new ones are added.

## How it teaches

- **Rehearsal before each take.** The first time you record a track, a short rehearsal teaches what's new. Each new note, rhythm, shape or theory idea gets a card with an example, how it sounds, and how it connects to what you already know. Cards come two at a time, each pair followed by a quick check with no penalty, then a mixed check before you record. Tracks with nothing new get a quick warm-up instead.
- **Recording a take.** A take is 10 to 15 questions. The tape winds from reel to reel as you answer. Each mistake lights a red **CLIP** lamp, and three clips ruin the take. Finished takes are stamped **Master take** (no clips), **Keeper** (one) or **Rough take**. Missed questions come back at the end.
- **Spaced repetition, built into practice.** Everything you answer gets a next-review date: tomorrow, then about 3 days, 8, 20 and so on. Intervals stretch less if you were slow, and a miss comes back tomorrow. Answering before it's due doesn't count, so cramming can't fake it. Due material from earlier tapes is mixed into takes (3 of 10 questions, tagged "↺ Review · from tape N"), and missing one costs no clip. There's no separate daily chore.
- **Speed-based mastery.** Every answer is timed against a fluency target, for example about 2s to name a note on the neck and 4s to name a note in a scale. Each note or idea is *learning* until you get it right reliably, then *accurate*, and *fluent* only when you're right **and** fast. Fluency fades if something goes long past its review date. Slow answers come up more often, just like wrong ones.

These follow well-studied learning principles: retrieval practice (recalling instead of rereading), spacing, interleaving (mixing old and new material), small steps with worked examples, feedback that explains every answer, and practicing to fluency rather than stopping at "correct".

## Exercises

- **Reading pitch.** Name a note on the staff or find where it's played; accidentals and the measure rule; key signatures (name the key, or name or find a note in it); steps, skips and leaps; short phrases played in order on the fretboard.
- **Reading rhythm.** Name a note value or rest, count its beats, complete a measure, tap the note on a given count, and **tap a rhythm** in time after a one-measure count-in, on the big pad or with Space on a laptop. The metronome is a click or, on phones, a vibration.
- **The fretboard.** Name a marked note, find a note on one string or everywhere on the neck, and tap an interval or octave above a root.
- **Theory.** Half or whole step, on the neck or the staff; the notes of any major or natural minor scale, spelled correctly; scale degrees in patterns on the neck; how many sharps or flats a key has; relative major and minor keys; naming intervals from note names or the staff; turning intervals upside down.
- **Ear training.** Hear two notes, rising, falling or together, and name the interval, or tap where the second note is on the neck. Each question plays as it appears, with **▶ Hear again**. Intervals start very different (octave or 5th?) and narrow down to ones that sound alike, with familiar melodies on the lesson cards as memory aids.

## The app

- **Tapes (home).** A tape deck holding the tape with your next track (marked **Now playing**), with the rack below. Tap a tape to load it into the deck. **⏏ Load tape** opens its page, with the track list and liner notes (tips), and **▶ Play** starts the next track.
- **Jam.** **Review now** for spare minutes; a 60-second sprint with a personal best for each kind (Keys and Intervals sprints unlock as you learn them); **Trouble spots**, twelve questions on whatever you miss most or answer slowest, fretboard and theory alike; **Theory jam**, one-tap mixes from the theory tracks you've recorded, which appears once you reach Vol. 3; and a custom **Jam setup** for fretboard and staff drills.
- **Fretboard.** A reference neck. Tap any fret to hear it and see where it's written on the staff, or **solo** one note to see everywhere it appears.
- **Meters.** Totals on LCD readouts, plus a signal map of the fretboard: red is still learning, amber is right but slow, green is fluent.
- **Settings.** Grouped as Sound (sound, vibration, metronome, ear training), Lessons (auto-advance, unsealing every tape, daily goal), Fretboard (left-handed mode, string order when the neck is sideways) and Your tapes (backup and restore).
- **Monitors.** Ear training needs headphones. On any listening question, **Can’t listen now** pauses listening questions for 15 minutes, after which they come back by themselves; the **MONITORS** switch on the ear-training tape turns them back on sooner. While they're off, ear-training tracks wait and listening review is skipped. Turning ear training off in Settings skips those tracks for good.

The fretboard stands upright like a chord chart when the phone is upright, and turns sideways like tab when you rotate it.

## Privacy

Commuter Tapes has no accounts, analytics or tracking, and makes no network requests beyond loading the app itself. Progress is stored in your browser's local storage, so each device and browser keeps its own, and clearing the browser's site data erases it. To keep a copy or move to another device, use **Settings → Export backup** and **Load backup**. The backup is a file you keep; nothing is uploaded.

## Tech

- React, TypeScript and Vite, styled with Tailwind CSS
- [VexFlow](https://www.vexflow.com/) for sheet music. The fretboard is a hand-built SVG.
- Guitar tones synthesized in the browser (Karplus–Strong), so there are no audio files
- Zustand for state, saved to local storage
- Installable PWA with a custom service worker for offline use
- Vitest and Testing Library: music logic, every track in the curriculum, and a click-through of the whole app

## Run locally

Requires Node.js 22.22+ or 24.15+.

```sh
git clone https://github.com/christinatrujillo/commuter-tapes.git
cd commuter-tapes
npm install
npm run dev
```

| Command | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `npm run dev` | Start the dev server (add `-- --host` to open it from a phone on the same Wi-Fi) |
| `npm test` | Run the test suite |
| `npm run typecheck` | Type-check without building |
| `npm run build` | Type-check and build into `dist/` |
| `npm run preview` | Serve the production build locally |

Installing the app and using it offline need HTTPS, so they work on a deployed copy but not on the dev server.

## Deploy your own

Pushes to `main` run the tests, build the site and publish it to GitHub Pages (`.github/workflows/deploy.yml`). If you fork the repo, turn on **Settings → Pages → Source: GitHub Actions**. The build uses relative paths, so it works at any URL.

## Project structure

```
src/
  music/        notes and fretboard math, rhythm, reading rules and keys (score.ts), scales and intervals (theory.ts), and the curriculum (curriculum.ts)
  game/         question generation, rehearsal cards, theory and interval questions and cards, spaced-repetition review and trouble spots, jams and drills
  components/   fretboard, staff and score, rhythm notation, tap pad, circle of fifths, monitors switch, cassette and studio-hardware UI
  screens/      home (deck and rack), tape page, rehearsal, recording a take, sprint, jam room, fretboard, meters, settings
  store/        saved progress, review schedule, streaks and settings
  audio/        plucked-string synthesis and metronome
  lib/          hash router, plus date, color, random-number and text helpers
  test-helpers.ts  helpers shared by the tests
public/         icons and web app manifest
.github/        GitHub Pages deploy workflow
```

**Adding content:** tapes and their tracks are defined in `TAPES` in `src/music/curriculum.ts`, and `VOLUMES` places them on the rack and lists the planned ones. Theory tracks list the ideas they teach in `topics`; each idea's card and quick checks are in `src/game/theory.ts` (intervals and ear training in `src/game/intervals.ts`). Keep track ids unchanged once released, since saved progress is keyed by them. The tests check that every track produces valid questions and that no track reads in a key before its key signature is taught. Fluency targets are set in `FLUENT_MS` in `src/game/questions.ts`.
