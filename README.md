# Commuter Tapes

**Read music, know every note on the guitar, and learn music theory, in short lessons you can finish between train stops.**

**[▶ Try it in your browser](https://christinatrujillo.github.io/commuter-tapes/)**. On Android, open it in Chrome and choose **Install app**. Once installed, it works offline.

https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/47038d3a-b0a9-4cb0-a2e4-f165bac6b280

Commuter Tapes is set in an analog recording studio. Each topic is a cassette on your tape rack, each lesson is a track you record, and the tapes are grouped into volumes, one per chapter. Your progress shows up on VU meters, CLIP lights and LCD readouts. There are no accounts and no ads, and nothing leaves your phone.

## Curriculum

There's one path through the rack. Reading follows method-book order: the staff and note values first, then first position string by string from the high E string down, with a new rhythm idea on each string. Fretboard tapes take each string up the neck once it's been read. From Volume 3 on, the volumes follow the usual order of a guitar theory course.

| Volume | What it covers | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Vol. 1 · Reading & the Neck** | The staff, note values and counting in 4/4 and 3/4; rests, eighth notes, ties and dotted notes; reading all six strings in first position; the high E, low E and A strings up the neck | Available |
| **Vol. 2 · The Whole Neck** | Sharps, flats and naturals, and how long an accidental lasts; the D, G and B strings up the neck; the whole neck, naturals and accidentals; reading by steps, skips and leaps, and short phrases; 2/4 and cut time; reading up the neck; octaves | Available |
| **Vol. 3 · Scales & Keys** | Whole and half steps; the major scale (its steps, scale degrees and spelling in any key); major scale patterns on the neck; key signatures; the circle of fifths, all 15 keys; relative minor keys and the natural minor scale | Available |
| **Vol. 4 · Intervals** | Interval shapes on the neck; naming intervals by number and quality (major, minor, perfect, augmented, diminished); intervals on the staff, in a key and stacked; inversions; ear training, from octaves and 5ths down to 2nds and the tritone | Available |
| **Vol. 5 · Triads** | What a triad is; major, minor, diminished and augmented triads, spelled and told apart by their 3rds; triads on the staff, in a key; inversions; three-string triad shapes on the neck; hearing the four kinds | Available |
| **Vol. 6 · Chords in a Key** | The triad on every note of the major scale and the pattern they follow; Roman numerals; I, IV and V, what each does and where they sit on the neck; common progressions (I–IV–V–I, I–V–vi–IV, I–vi–IV–V, ii–V–I); hearing where a progression goes | Available |
| **Vol. 7 · Building Chords** | Chord symbols; major 7th, dominant 7th, minor 7th and half-diminished chords, and the 7th chords in a key; sus, add9 and 6 chords; reading chord diagrams and the open chords; the CAGED shapes up the neck; hearing 7th chords | Available |
| **Vol. 8 · Minor Keys** | The chords in a minor key and their Roman numerals; harmonic minor, its raised 7th, the major V and V7, and the diminished 7th; minor progressions (i–iv–v–i, i–VI–VII–i, i–iv–V7–i, ii°–V7–i); finding the key from the key signature and the chords; hearing major or minor, and minor v or major V | Available |
| **Vol. 9 · Blues & Pentatonics** | The minor and major pentatonic scales and how they share notes; the blues scale and its blue note; the five pentatonic patterns on the neck, their roots and the CAGED shape inside each; the 12-bar blues, in numerals and in a key, with the quick change, the turnaround and minor blues; hearing major or minor pentatonic, the blue note and the quick change | Available |

Planned tapes sit on the rack as blank **Coming soon** cassettes, numbered in order with the rest. Recorded tapes keep their numbers; a planned volume's blanks are placeholders, so the numbers after it can shift when it's recorded with more tapes.

## How it teaches

- **Rehearsal before each take.** The first time you record a track, a short rehearsal teaches what's new. Each new note, rhythm, shape or theory idea gets a card with an example, how it sounds, and how it connects to what you already know. Cards come two at a time, each pair followed by a quick check with no penalty, then a mixed check before you record. Tracks with nothing new get a quick warm-up instead.
- **Recording a take.** A take is 6 to 15 questions, usually 10 or 12. The tape winds from reel to reel as you answer. Each mistake lights a red **CLIP** lamp, and three clips ruin the take. Finished takes are stamped **Master take** (no clips), **Keeper** (one) or **Rough take**. Missed questions come back at the end.
- **Spaced repetition, built into practice.** Everything you answer gets a next-review date: tomorrow, then about 3 days, 8, 20 and so on. Intervals stretch less if you were slow, and a miss comes back tomorrow. Answering before it's due doesn't count, so cramming can't fake it. Due material from earlier tapes is mixed into takes (3 of 10 questions, tagged "↺ Review · from tape N"), and missing one costs no clip. There's no separate daily chore.
- **Speed-based mastery.** Every answer is timed against a fluency target, for example about 2s to name a note on the neck and 4s to name a note in a scale. Each note or idea is *learning* until you get it right reliably, then *accurate*, and *fluent* only when you're right **and** fast. Fluency fades if something goes long past its review date. Slow answers come up more often, just like wrong ones.
- **Words explained where you meet them.** Music words on cards, in feedback and in liner notes are underlined; tap one and a small liner-note card gives its meaning in a sentence or two.

These follow well-studied learning principles: retrieval practice (recalling instead of rereading), spacing, interleaving (mixing old and new material), small steps with worked examples, feedback that explains every answer, and practicing to fluency rather than stopping at "correct".

## Exercises

- **Reading pitch.** Name a note on the staff or find where it's played; accidentals and the measure rule; key signatures (name the key, or name or find a note in it); steps, skips and leaps; short phrases played in order on the fretboard.
- **Reading rhythm.** Name a note value or rest, count its beats, complete a measure, tap the note on a given count, and **tap a rhythm** in time after a one-measure count-in, on the big pad or with Space on a laptop. The metronome is a click or, on phones, a vibration.
- **The fretboard.** Name a marked note, find a note on one string or everywhere on the neck, and tap an interval or octave above a root.
- **Theory.** Half or whole step, on the neck or the staff; the notes of any major or natural minor scale, spelled correctly; scale degrees in patterns on the neck; how many sharps or flats a key has, and the next key around the circle; relative major and minor keys; naming intervals from note names or the staff; turning intervals upside down; spelling all four kinds of triad and telling them apart; naming triads on the staff, in a key and inverted; naming triad shapes on the neck and tapping a shape's missing note; the chords in any major key, by name and Roman numeral; I, IV and V, and their roots on the neck; spelling and naming common progressions; reading and writing chord symbols; spelling 7th, sus, add9 and 6 chords and telling them apart; the 7th chords in a key; naming chords from chord diagrams and the CAGED shapes; the chords in any minor key; harmonic minor's raised 7th, V7 and diminished 7th; spelling and naming minor progressions; telling whether a song is in the major key or its relative minor; spelling the minor and major pentatonic and the blues scale; the five pentatonic patterns, their roots and the CAGED shape inside each; reading a 12-bar blues chart, its chords in a key, the quick change, the turnaround and minor blues.
- **Ear training.** Hear two notes, rising, falling or together, and name the interval, or tap where the second note is on the neck. Hear a triad, one note at a time or together, and name its kind. Hear a progression and tell whether it ends at home, whether the second chord is IV or V, or which progression it is. Hear a 7th chord and name its kind. Hear a progression and tell whether its key is major or minor, or whether a minor key's V chord is minor or major. Hear a scale and tell major pentatonic from minor, or whether it has the blue note; hear the start of a blues and tell whether it makes the quick change. Each question plays as it appears, with **▶ Hear again**. Intervals start very different (octave or 5th?) and narrow down to ones that sound alike, with familiar melodies on the lesson cards as memory aids.

## The app

- **Tapes (home).** A tape deck holding the tape with your next track (marked **Now playing**), with the rack below: a wooden shelf per volume, each tape lit with a green light per recorded track. Tap a tape to load it into the deck; a sealed one shows there which tape to finish first. **⏏ Load tape** opens its page, with the track list and liner notes (tips), and **▶ Play** starts the next track.
- **Jam.** **Review now** for spare minutes; a 60-second sprint with a personal best for each kind (Keys and Intervals sprints unlock as you learn them); **Trouble spots**, twelve questions on whatever you miss most or answer slowest, fretboard and theory alike; **Theory jam**, one-tap mixes from the theory tracks you've recorded (steps and scales, keys, intervals, triads, chords in a key, building chords, minor keys, blues and pentatonics, and ear training), which appears once you reach Vol. 3; and a custom **Jam setup** for fretboard and staff drills.
- **Fretboard.** A reference neck. Show the natural notes only, or every note named with **sharps** or with **flats** (the same frets under their two names). Tap a note to hear it and see where it's written on the staff, or **solo** one note to see everywhere it appears. Once you reach the scale and interval tapes, a **Scale** view lights up any key you've learned (with note names or degrees) and an **Interval** view shows every spot an interval above a note you tap. The Scale view adds the major and minor pentatonic and the blues scale as you learn them, and the five pentatonic patterns one at a time.
- **Meters.** Fluent spots and accuracy on LCD readouts (your streak and note count are on the strip at the top of every tab); a level bar per skill (fretboard, reading, rhythm, scales and keys, intervals, triads, chords, ear) showing how much of what you've practiced is fluent; and a signal map of the fretboard: red is still learning, amber is right but slow, green is fluent.
- **Settings.** Grouped as Sound (sound, vibration, metronome, ear training), Lessons (auto-advance, unsealing every tape, daily goal), Fretboard (left-handed mode, string order when the neck is sideways) and Your tapes (backup and restore).
- **Monitors.** Ear training needs headphones, so ear-training tapes and tracks are marked with a pair of them. On any listening question, **Can’t listen now** pauses listening questions for 15 minutes, after which they come back by themselves; the **MONITORS** switch on each ear-training tape's page turns them back on sooner. While they're off, ear-training tracks wait and listening review is skipped. Turning ear training off in Settings skips those tracks for good.

The fretboard stands upright like a chord chart when the phone is upright, and turns sideways like tab when you rotate it.

## Privacy

Commuter Tapes has no accounts, analytics or tracking, and makes no network requests beyond loading the app itself. Progress is stored in your browser's local storage, so each device and browser keeps its own, and clearing the browser's site data erases it. To keep a copy or move to another device, use **Settings → Export backup** and **Load backup**. The backup is a file you keep; nothing is uploaded.

## Tech

- React, TypeScript and Vite, styled with Tailwind CSS
- [VexFlow](https://www.vexflow.com/) for sheet music. The fretboard is a hand-built SVG.
- Guitar tones synthesized in the browser (Karplus–Strong), so there are no audio files
- Zustand for state, saved to local storage
- Installable PWA with a custom service worker for offline use
- Vitest and Testing Library: music logic, every track in the curriculum, and a click-through of the whole app

## Run locally

Requires Node.js 22.22+ or 24.15+.

```sh
git clone https://github.com/christinatrujillo/commuter-tapes.git
cd commuter-tapes
npm install
npm run dev
```

| Command | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `npm run dev` | Start the dev server (add `-- --host` to open it from a phone on the same Wi-Fi) |
| `npm test` | Run the test suite |
| `npm run typecheck` | Type-check without building |
| `npm run build` | Type-check and build into `dist/` |
| `npm run preview` | Serve the production build locally |

Installing the app and using it offline need HTTPS, so they work on a deployed copy but not on the dev server.

## Deploy your own

Pushes to `main` run the tests, build the site and publish it to GitHub Pages (`.github/workflows/deploy.yml`). If you fork the repo, turn on **Settings → Pages → Source: GitHub Actions**. The build uses relative paths, so it works at any URL.

## Project structure

```
src/
  music/        notes and fretboard math, rhythm, reading rules and keys (score.ts), scales, intervals, triads, chords and the chords in a key (theory.ts), chord shapes (chordShapes.ts), and the curriculum (curriculum.ts)
  game/         question generation, rehearsal cards, theory, interval, triad, chords-in-a-key, chord-building, minor-key and blues questions and cards, the glossary, spaced-repetition review and trouble spots, jams and drills
  components/   fretboard, staff and score, rhythm notation, tap pad, circle of fifths, chord diagrams and chord charts, monitors switch, glossary popups, cassette and studio-hardware UI
  screens/      home (deck and rack), tape page, rehearsal, recording a take, sprint, jam room, fretboard, meters, settings
  store/        saved progress, review schedule, streaks and settings
  audio/        plucked-string synthesis and metronome
  lib/          hash router, plus date, color, random-number and text helpers
  test-helpers.ts  helpers shared by the tests
public/         icons and web app manifest
.github/        GitHub Pages deploy workflow
```

**Adding content:** tapes and their tracks are defined in `TAPES` in `src/music/curriculum.ts`, and `VOLUMES` places them on the rack and lists the planned ones. Theory tracks list the ideas they teach in `topics`; each idea's card and quick checks are in `src/game/theory.ts` (intervals and ear training in `src/game/intervals.ts`, triads in `src/game/triads.ts`, chords in a key and progressions in `src/game/harmony.ts`, building chords in `src/game/chords.ts`, minor keys in `src/game/minor.ts`, blues and pentatonics in `src/game/blues.ts`). Keep track ids unchanged once released, since saved progress is keyed by them. The tests check that every track produces valid questions and that no track reads in a key before its key signature is taught. Fluency targets are set in `FLUENT_MS` in `src/game/questions.ts`.
